HEM-ELITE-RACK — HOW TO USE

1. Start HEM-Elite-Rack (desktop shortcut).
2. OPEN SONG (top) — or drag a WAV / MP3 / FLAC onto the window.
   Songs at other sample rates are converted to 48 kHz when they load.
3. + ADD PLUG-IN (left) — pick any plug-in (tabs: EQ, DYNAMICS, COLOR, SPACE, MOVEMENT, TOOLS, KEYS). Up to 8 plug-ins, top to bottom.
   Each slot: ON / OFF, UP / DOWN (order), DEL (remove). Click a slot to show its faceplate.
4. PLAY (or the SPACE bar). Click the song strip to jump; LEFT / RIGHT arrows jump 5 s.
5. A PROCESSED / B ORIGINAL (or the A and B keys) — compare with and without the chain.
   MATCH LOUDNESS (on by default) brings B to the same loudness as A, so you judge the
   sound, not the volume.
6. EXPORT WAV — the whole song through the chain, 24-bit / 48 kHz. The status line then
   shows the file's integrated loudness (LUFS) and peak.
7. LIVE INPUT (a mic, a guitar) — needs an ASIO sound card with inputs (choose it with OUTPUT).
   INPUT steps through OFF, IN 1+2 (a stereo pair), IN 1, IN 2 (one input on both sides), and so on.
   With an input on, the chain works on your input instead of the song, and the song plays
   alongside, untouched, so you can sing or play over it. MONITOR: hear yourself through the chain
   (turn it off if your interface already lets you hear yourself). The bar under them shows your
   input level: keep it out of the red.
   RECORD starts the song (if it isn't playing) and records; STOP REC (or STOP) ends the take. Each take
   is saved twice in Documents\HEM-Elite-Rack Takes: "Take 001 ... .wav" (through the chain) and
   "Take 001 ... (dry).wav" (your input as it came in), 24-bit / 48 kHz, named with where in the song it
   started and lined up with the song from that point (the sound card's delay is taken out).
8. MIDI KEYBOARDS — any MIDI keyboard plugged into the PC is picked up within a few seconds and plays
   the instruments in the chain (GRAND LARCENY); its sustain pedal works.

SURGEON
  Double-click the graph to add a band where you clicked. Drag a dot: frequency and gain.
  Mouse wheel on a dot: width (Q). Double-click a dot: band off. Bands 1-16 buttons pick a
  band; its TYPE, STEREO (stereo / mid / side / left / right), SLOPE, FREQ, GAIN, Q are
  under the graph. DYNAMIC: the band only moves (up to RANGE) when that part of the song
  goes over THRESHOLD. CHARACTER: CLEAN, CONSOLE or TUBE colour, with DRIVE.
  AUTO GAIN keeps the loudness where it was so louder never fools you.

THE CLEANER
  NOISE REDUCTION: how hard. FOCUS: lean on the lows (hum, rumble) or the highs (hiss).
  AIR PRESERVE: protects the top end and the attacks. FREQUENCY RANGE: drag the two
  handles to work only between them. ADAPT: keeps learning the noise (off = freeze it).
  LISTEN: hear only what is being taken out.

THE FAMILY
  A mix-bus compressor: a few dB on the needle pulls a mix together. THRESHOLD, RATIO (2, 4,
  10:1), ATTACK, RELEASE (AUTO waits longer after the mix has been working it hard), MAKEUP,
  MIX (lower it for parallel compression), SIDECHAIN HPF (60 / 90 / 150 Hz: the kick and bass
  stop making the whole mix duck).

ARCHIVE
  INPUT drives the tape (AUTO TRIM keeps the level the same, so you hear the colour, not the
  volume). SPEED 15 IPS = more low-end bump and a softer top, 30 IPS = tighter and brighter.
  HEAD BUMP, WOW (pitch drift), HISS. VU CAL picks what reads 0 on the meters: -18 dBFS for quiet
  mixes, -10 (the default) or -6 for loud finished songs.

VELVET
  LOW: pick a frequency, BOOST and/or ATTEN. Both together = the classic trick: more weight at
  the very bottom and less mud just above. HIGH: frequency, BOOST, WIDTH (sharp .. broad).
  HIGH ATTENUATION: SELECT 5 / 10 / 20 kHz and ATTEN to soften the top. TUBE: DRIVE, OUTPUT.
  EQ switches the EQ out (to compare; the tube stays). ANALOG adds the old box's hum (50 or 60 Hz).

FOUNDRY
  The console channel. Each band has a ring that picks its frequency (OFF = the band is out) and
  a knob for how much (-18 .. +18 dB): LOWS (a shelf), MIDDLE (a bell), HIGHS (a shelf). LOW CUT
  takes out rumble below its frequency, steeply. EQ OUT = the curves go flat (VINTAGE stays). FLIP
  turns the sound upside down (for a second mic out of phase). VINTAGE adds the old desk's warmth
  and a little hiss. The windows show the PEAK level out, TO TOP = how far OUTPUT can go up before
  the peak reaches the top, and CLIPS; CLEAR resets them.

BOARDROOM
  The bigger console EQ, four bands. HIGH-PASS and LOW-PASS cut the extremes. LOWS and HIGHS are
  shelves, or bells with LOWS BELL / HIGHS BELL. LOW MIDS and HIGH MIDS are bells: wide reaches
  12 dB at the end of the knob, TIGHT is narrower and reaches 18. EQ, FLIP, VINTAGE, OUTPUT and the
  readouts work as on FOUNDRY.

MIDTOWN
  The middle of the mix. LOW PEAK and HIGH PEAK lift a band (pick its frequency on the ring next
  to it), DIP cuts one; all three can be used together. 10 on LOW PEAK or DIP = 10 dB, on HIGH PEAK
  8 dB. EQ OUT to compare, HUM adds the tube box's hum (50 or 60 Hz).

REEL MONEY
  A tube tape machine. INTO TAPE is how hard you hit it (with LINK on, OFF TAPE takes the level back
  down so you hear the colour, not the volume). TAPE LEVEL (185 = normal) pushes the tape itself
  harder. SPEED 15 IPS is cleaner and brighter, 7.5 darker with more low bump. BIAS HOT = smoother
  top, NORMAL = brighter and a bit dirtier. WOBBLE (50 = a healthy machine). HISS (OFF all the way down).
  The echo comes off the tape: ECHO LEVEL (OFF = none), ECHO TIME, SLAP (one repeat) or REPEATS, ECHO
  TONE. MONITOR INPUT = only the tube stage before the tape. The VU shows IN or OUT; VU ZERO sets what
  reads 0.

PAWN SHOP
  Four modules, each with its own switch. DEVICE: SOUND (the band of an old radio, phone or speaker),
  TONE (darker to brighter), SQUASH (minus squeezes, plus pushes the loud parts out), RINGER (ring
  modulation and digital clipping), DECADE (the 50s to the 80s), MIX. SPACE: the echo (SYNC to a BPM,
  to the song, or MS; NOTE or TIME; ECHO level; PING PONG; FEEDBACK: 100 repeats forever) and the
  reverb (SPRING or PLATE, LEVEL, LENGTH), SPACE MIX. NOISE: twelve kinds (we make them; the original
  plays recordings), FOLLOWS DUCK (quieter while the music plays) or GATE (only with the music) above
  THRESHOLD (-60 = always on), PLACE PRE (into the space) or POST, LEVEL. MECHANICS: WOW (pitch),
  WOBBLE (level) and SPEED for A (tape-like) and B (record-like). Then INPUT, OUTPUT, LOW CUT, HIGH CUT
  and MONO (10 = the stereo as it was).

HOTHEAD
  One knob: DRIVE. 0 leaves the sound alone; up from there a light overdrive turns into full
  distortion, darker as it goes, at about the same level.

MIXTAPE
  A cassette deck. DRIVE pushes the tape, TAPE picks TYPE I (darkest, saturates first), II or IV
  (brightest, cleanest). WOW = slow pitch drift, FLUTTER = fast, HISS, AGE = a worn, duller tape.

CRATE DIGGER
  A record player. CRACKLE = clicks and pops, NOISE = surface hiss and rumble, WEAR = duller with
  a little grit, WARP = the pitch wobble of a bent record. MONO LOWS puts the deep bass in the middle.

SHREDDER
  Lo-fi. BITS down for grit (8 and under is obvious), DOWNSAMPLE up for the metallic sound of a
  low sample rate. MIX blends it with the clean sound.

LOCKDOWN (put it last in the chain)
  INPUT pushes the song into it: louder. CEILING is the most that ever comes out (-1 dB is a
  safe choice for streaming). TRUE PEAK on keeps the peaks *between* samples under the ceiling
  too. RELEASE (or AUTO), LINK. The readouts show the loudness (LUFS) and the true peak of what
  comes out; the true peak turns red if it is over the ceiling.

WIDE LOAD
  WIDTH 100 % = as it was, 0 % = mono, up to 200 %. MONO BASS keeps the low end in the centre
  (punchier, safe on club systems and phones). CORRELATION: +1 = mono, 0 = wide,
  below 0 = parts cancel in mono (be careful). SHUFFLE widens the lows and low mids (under SHUFFLE
  FREQ) for a bigger bottom, BASS TRIM sets the middle's low end under it; ROTATION turns the whole
  picture left or right; ASYMMETRY slides the middle over without moving the edges (fixes a lopsided
  mix). POLARITY L / R flip a side, SWAP swaps them, INPUT M / S for a track recorded mid / side;
  METER L / R or M / S picks what the two level readouts show.

SPARK
  FREQUENCY = above where new harmonics are added; nothing below it changes. TUBE is warmer,
  TAPE edgier. DRIVE = how much grit, MIX = how much is added. A little goes a long way.

ENFORCER
  The all-rounder. THRESHOLD down until the needle moves, RATIO (2 - 4 gentle, 8+ hard), KNEE
  (higher = smoother), ATTACK (slower lets the punch through), RELEASE or AUTO REL,
  AUTO GAIN gives back about half of what it takes. SIDECHAIN HPF stops the bass pumping it.
  TAIL LINGER eases the release out slowly at the end; CHARACTER WARM adds low-end weight as it works.

HITMAN (dual FET peak compressor)
  Turn INPUT up to compress harder, OUTPUT to set the level (OUTPUT also drives SATURATION).
  ATTACK and RELEASE: 7 is fastest. RATIO 4 / 8 / 12 / 20, or STACKED (every ratio at once) for the aggressive, crunchy smash.
  MODE L/R or M/S; LINK makes channel 2 follow channel 1's knobs (turn it off to set them apart).
  LOOKAHEAD (up to 2 ms) catches the very first edge of a hit. SC EQ shapes what it listens to
  (e.g. SC HPF so the kick doesn't pump it); LISTEN lets you hear that. SOURCE EXT uses the
  sidechain input in a DAW (the rack has no second input, so there it listens to itself).
  SATURATION: DRY, CLIP (nothing over 0 dBFS), WARM, TRIODE, TAPE. MIX for parallel smashing.
  The screen on the right shows the last few seconds: gain reduction of both channels and the input.

SWITCHBLADE
  A compressor and a gate on one plate, plus a filter. Compressor row: THRESHOLD, RATIO (under 1 lifts
  the loud parts; CANCEL makes the loudest parts come out quieter), ATTACK, RELEASE, SMART REL (short peaks
  let go at SMART REL instead: punchier), MAKEUP; LEVEL REF LOW is the classic, PEAK keeps full-scale peaks
  where they were. Gate row: GATE OPEN, CLOSE (under OPEN: no chatter), FLOOR (how far down; -80 = all
  the way), SLOPE (EXPANDER mode), ATTACK, RELEASE, HOLD; LINK gives the gate the compressor's times.
  Each row's EQ: WIDE (the whole sound), SIDECHAIN (it only hears the FILTER), SPLIT (it only works on
  the filter's band: a dynamic EQ). FILTER BAND / NOTCH / LO CUT / HI CUT, FREQ, Q. MONITOR SIDECHAIN
  plays what the detector hears, PASSIVE the part left alone. KEY L > R: the left side's level works the
  right side (the left passes). INPUT L / R and OUTPUT down the left.

TERRITORY / STRAIGHT EDGE
  Each band: THRESHOLD, GAIN, RANGE (minus = that band comes down when it's loud, plus = it gets lifted,
  0 = no movement; GAIN still works as an EQ), ATTACK, RELEASE, SOLO, BYPASS. The MASTER column moves
  every band at once. SPLIT knobs move the borders. KNEE 0 = soft and wide, 100 = hard. RELEASE AUTO
  follows the music; LET-GO EASE OUT lets go slowly at the end, SNAP BACK quickly. TERRITORY: Q = how steep the steps
  between bands are. STRAIGHT EDGE (no phase shift, 53 ms late): MAKEUP AUTO gives each band back half
  its RANGE, ADAPTIVE lets a band work only when it stands out over the band under it, TRIM (one push)
  brings the loudest peak since the last push to 0 dBFS, DITHER for 24-bit.

THE HARDWARE-STYLE COMPRESSORS - most have a METER switch: IN and OUT show VU level on the needle
(0 VU = -18 dBFS), GR shows how much it's turning down. HUM 50 or 60 HZ adds the faint hum
of the hardware under the music (never on silence).

CONSIGLIERE / STONE COLD
  SQUEEZE (how much) and GAIN (level back up), or AUTO MAKEUP to keep the level put. MODE
  COMPRESS or LIMIT. BRIGHT DETECT makes it react more to the highs. MIX for parallel, TRIM after it.
  CONSIGLIERE is smooth and slow; STONE COLD is quicker and firmer, with a bit of grit.

HEAVYWEIGHT
  THRESHOLD, RATIO, ATTACK, RELEASE (it makes up the level by itself). SNAP lets the hits through
  while the body is held. RELEASE IN: MS (the RELEASE knob), HOST (the song's tempo, with NOTE) or
  BPM (the BPM knob, with NOTE). VINTAGE 1-4 adds weight and grit (4 has no hiss). LIMITER on rounds
  peaks under -0.3 dBFS; off, a clipper stops them at 0 dBFS.

THE DON
  The old broadcast bus sound: it listens to its own output, so it breathes. THRESHOLD steps down
  until the needle moves, pick RATIO and DECAY (1 = fast, 32 = slow). VU ZERO sets where 0 VU
  sits (and so where the threshold sits).

SLOW BURN / LONE WOLF
  Tube limiting that gets firmer the harder you push. INPUT at -14 = no level change; THRESHOLD up
  from 0 to start compressing. TIMING 1-6 sets the speed (5 and 6 let go by themselves).
  SLOW BURN's LINK: SEPARATE, LINKED or MID / SIDE. COMPRESS OFF leaves just the tubes.

IRON FIST
  Like a console channel: turn INPUT up to compress harder, OUTPUT to set the level (AUTO MAKEUP
  does that for you). RATIO, RELEASE (AUTO follows the music), DE-ESS makes it react only to esses.
  LIMITER with LIMIT AT / ATTACK / RELEASE catches the peaks. VINTAGE = the output iron and hum.

QUICK DRAW
  More INPUT = more limiting (-30 is where it starts); OUTPUT sets the level (-18 = no change).
  ATTACK and RELEASE: 7 is the fastest. RATIO 4 to 20, or STACKED (every ratio at once) for the over-the-top smash.
  ERA EARLY colours more than LATE.

OLD MONEY
  A clean, smooth all-rounder. RATIO under 1 makes loud parts louder. RELEASE AUTO follows the
  music; SNAP BACK lets go quicker (more level), EASE OUT smoother. COLOR COLORED adds a touch of colour, PURE stays clean. OUTPUT has
  a soft limiter after it so it never goes over.

KNOCKOUT
  ATTACK + makes hits snap, - softens them. SUSTAIN + brings up the ring and room, - dries it.
  PICKUP and LENGTH for each, CURVE for the feel. SAFETY CLIP / LIMIT keeps it under 0 dBFS.

HAYMAKER / FOUR KINGS
  RANGE + makes hits stand out, - softens them; PICKUP = how big a jump counts as a hit; LENGTH = how
  fast a rise must be; RELEASE = how quickly it lets go. FOUR KINGS does it in four bands (each with
  ON, RANGE, PICKUP; MASTER moves them all). TRIM (one push) brings the loudest peak to 0 dBFS.

BACKBONE
  Two faders. QUIET UP = the quiet parts come up (it lifts noise too, so go easy). LOUD DOWN =
  the loud parts are held, with the level made back up. OUTPUT trims the end result.

FULL HOUSE
  Four sections, each with its own ON. EVENER brings loud passages toward its level, slowly (STRONG lets
  go sooner). GATE keeps quiet noise down. LOW lifts what's under its THRESHOLD by up to GAIN.
  HIGH holds what's over its THRESHOLD; its GAIN is the most that ever comes out.

WHEELMAN / CHAUFFEUR
  Set TARGET to the level you want the vocal (WHEELMAN) or bass (CHAUFFEUR) to sit at; it rides the
  level between LOWEST RIDE and HIGHEST RIDE. REST POINT = where it sits when the part isn't playing. WHEELMAN: VOICE
  PICKUP = how loud the voice must be to count; STAGE + BLEED FLOOR ignores other instruments in the mic.
  CHAUFFEUR: NOTE PICKUP, SPEED, IGNORE BLEED and IGNORE CLICKS decide what counts as a bass note.
  (No music sidechain and no automation writing: the rack doesn't have those.)

THE MAXIMIZERS - every one ends with the same three steppers:
  BIT DEPTH = the word length of what comes out (24 BIT unless the file is going to 16 bit).
  DITHER SOFT = plain, safe; FULL = less noise, a little grit at very low levels; NONE.
  NOISE SHAPE LIGHT / MEDIUM / STRONG moves that noise away from where the ear hears best (STRONG only
  on the very last step of a master); OFF. Silence stays silent.

BIG SHOT
  THRESHOLD down = louder; CEILING = nothing comes out above it. AUTO REL on (best for most things)
  lets go quickly after single peaks and slower when it's working hard; off = a steady 100 ms.

LAST WORD
  INPUT L / R trim each side first (INPUT LINK on: both follow INPUT L). THRESHOLD, CEILING, RELEASE
  (0.01 ms to 1 s), AUTO REL (quicker after single peaks, up to RELEASE when it works hard).
  PEAK MODE: SAMPLE = no sample above the ceiling; TRUE PEAK = not even between the samples (what
  streaming services check); SMOOTHED = like TRUE PEAK, rounded softly into the ceiling.

HIGH STAKES / FAST MONEY
  Five bands. THRESHOLD, CEILING, LINK (on: the ceiling comes down with the threshold, so you hear
  the limiting without the loudness going up), RELEASE (times every band's release), BAND FREEDOM
  (0 = all bands give the same, 100 = the band carrying the peak gives the most), the four SPLIT
  knobs between the bands. Per band: GAIN (pushes that band in harder), WEIGHT (that band gives up
  less, the others more), LINK (GAIN also raises WEIGHT: louder band, kept), RELEASE, SOLO.
  RELEASE stepper: AUTO, AUTO LOWS (adapts most in the lows), AUTO HIGHS, HARD (loudest), MANUAL
  (each band's own release). HIGH STAKES is linear phase (55 ms late, the host lines it up);
  FAST MONEY is 2 ms late, for tracking and live.

TOP DOG / QUICK CASH
  The five-band limiter driven by a PROFILE: STANDARD, OLD SCHOOL (thick, soft), HALF SPLIT,
  SINGLE AUTO / SINGLE FIXED (works as one band), VELVET (smooth, warm), STRIPPED (lean), QUIET
  LIFT (brings quiet parts up), HEAVY (loudest). THRESHOLD, CEILING, LINK, RELEASE. QUICK CASH = 2 ms late.

SWEET SIXTEEN
  Sixteen bands. Six EQ / priority columns: GAIN is an EQ before the limiter, WEIGHT uses the same
  curve to say where the limiting may come from, LINK moves them together, FREQ, Q, the type
  stepper (BELL / LOW SHELF / HIGH SHELF), IN. RELEASE FEEL = how the sixteen bands let go (22 of them:
  VOCAL UP lets the vocal range back quicker, HARSH DOWN keeps 2-6 kHz down longer, and so on);
  BAND FREEDOM LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH = how sharply the sixteen bands are cut apart. The lowest bands
  overlap (no linear-phase filter can cut sharply that low).

SILENCER / TIGHT LIPS
  THRESHOLD down until the esses get caught. FREQUENCY = where the esses are; SIDECHAIN HIGH-PASS
  listens to everything above it, BAND-PASS only around it. MODE BAND ONLY turns down only the highs, WHOLE the
  whole sound for that moment. TIGHT LIPS: RANGE = the most it takes off; LISTEN plays what it hears.
  SILENCER: MONITOR DETECTOR plays what it hears.

OMERTA
  No frequency to set: it hears how bright each sound is, so loud and soft esses at any pitch are
  caught the same. THRESHOLD, RANGE, CATCH WIDTH (0 = only pure esses), MODE from WHOLE to BAND,
  LOOKAHEAD catches each ess from its start. MONITOR plays what it takes out.

BREATHLESS
  LEVEL GATE: breaths are quieter than this. SENSITIVITY: how breath-like a sound must be.
  REDUCTION: how far they go down (all the way left = gone). SOFT END / START for smooth edges.
  ROOM FLOOR: breaths only go down to the room's level, so gaps don't go dead. LISTEN REMOVED plays only
  what it takes out.

TOMMY GUN
  One knob: AMOUNT (0-10). RATE = how often it pumps (a note of the tempo), OFFSET shifts where in the beat
  it dips. TEMPO FROM HOST in a DAW; BPM knob in the rack.

STRAIGHT SHOOTER
  An EQ that never shifts the phase: good on the mix bus and for mastering. The curve is on top; under
  it one column per band: TYPE, FREQ, GAIN, Q, and BAND (on / off). Band 1 is for the lows (cuts,
  shelves, a bell). Bands 2-6 have nine shapes: BELL; LO CUT / LO SHELF / HI SHELF / HI CUT, where Q
  sets how steep (never a bump); and the "BUMP" ones, where Q adds a bump at the corner.
  At the bottom: GAIN (the output), TRIM (press it after the loudest part has played: the output is
  set so that peak hits 0 dB), DITHER (only when you bounce to 24-bit). Left: FILTER (STANDARD, EXACT
  for the tightest curve, SMOOTH for the least ripple) and VIEW RANGE (the screen shows ±12 or ±30 dB).
  It delays the sound by 96 ms; DAWs make up for that by themselves, in the rack you won't notice.

BOTTOM LINE
  The same idea for the low end only: three bands from 11 to 600 Hz. It delays the sound by 181 ms
  (twice as long a filter, so the curve holds down low).

TEN TOES
  Ten bands, and the left and right side can each have their own. LEFT / RIGHT (on the left) pick
  which side the knobs show; with LINK on the right side simply follows the left. TIGHT BELL gets
  narrower the more you boost or cut. INPUT L / R (all the way left = off), PHASE L / R flip a side,
  OUTPUT L / R, TRIM as on STRAIGHT SHOOTER. The gold line on the screen is the right side, when it
  differs from the left.

FOUR CORNERS
  Four filters, left and right sets like TEN TOES. Filters 1 and 4 can be shelves, resonant shelves
  (Q adds a bump) or cuts; 2 and 3 are bells or shelves. GAIN L / GAIN R at the bottom.

SMOOTH TALKER
  Six bands, left and right sets. Band 1 is a low cut, a resonant low shelf or a bell; 2-3 low shelf or
  bell; 4-5 high shelf or bell; 6 a high cut, a resonant high shelf or a bell. SPECTRUM switches the
  music behind the curve on or off. OUTPUT (all the way left = off) and TRIM.

CUTTHROAT
  A high-pass (cuts the lows) and a low-pass (cuts the highs), each with its ON. LEFT / RIGHT pick the
  side the knobs show; with LINK on the right follows the left. The gold line is the right side.

TUNNEL VISION
  One knob: FILTER 10 = open, turn it down and the highs close in. RESONANCE adds a whistle at the edge.

THE SETUP
  A filter that moves by itself. Pick the TYPE (low / high / band pass, notch, comb, level) and set it,
  then give it a mover: the LFO (a wave), the SEQUENCER (16 steps, each up or down; LAST = how many) or
  the FOLLOWER (how loud the track is). Each mover has three depth knobs: > FREQ, > RES, > TIME (the
  delay). SYNC locks a mover to the tempo (pick the NOTE). GLIDE rounds off the steps. DRIVE, CRUSH
  (lo-fi) and SPREAD (left and right apart). The DELAY: TIME, FEEDBACK, DRY / WET, RIGHT (the right
  side later), STYLE WARM bends the pitch when the time moves, ROUTE IN FEEDBACK puts the filter
  inside the echoes.

THE CHAIRMAN
  A British console channel. SOURCE MIC or LINE; on MIC the PREAMP knob (20-70) drives the input
  stage harder for more colour (the level stays put). HIGH is a 10 kHz shelf in 4 dB steps, MID boosts
  or cuts (the MID button) at MID FREQ, LOW lifts at LOW FREQ, LOW CUT takes 50 Hz down. EQ switches the
  whole EQ in or out, PHASE flips it. HUM adds the hum of the old desk (50 or 60 Hz) and HISS says
  how much hiss. TRIM: press it after the loudest part; the output is set so that peak hits 0 dB.
  METER IN / OUT picks what the VU shows, VU ZERO how hot 0 VU is.

INSIDE MAN
  A whole channel: the gate (or expander) and the compressor side by side, the four-band EQ under them.
  KEY (on each) makes that section listen through a filter: KEY TYPE and KEY FREQ / WIDTH pick what it
  hears, KEY FROM MAIN or BEFORE EQ. Compressor TYPE VOCAL picks its own ratio; AUTO REL makes the release
  follow the music; a RATIO under 1 makes the loud parts louder. ORDER: EQ first or the dynamics
  first. PLACE turns the stereo picture. Nothing ever goes over 0 dB (the clip guard rounds it).

HEAVY HITTER
  Pick the DRUM first: it decides where THUMP (the low end) and TREBLE (the top) work and how SNAP
  hears a hit. SENSITIVITY is the level in (the INPUT window: aim for about -12 to -6 dBFS). SNAP pushes
  the front of every hit out. OUTPUT. METER picks what the LEVEL window shows: IN or OUT.

KINGPIN
  For drums. Pick the drum with TYPE first: it sets how the compressor moves and where BASS and TREBLE
  work. PICKUP = how hard the drum hits the compressor, COMPRESS = how much. GATE only on KICK (all the
  way down = off). AMBIENCE adds a short low boom on KICK, a bright plate on SNARE, a room on TOMS.

GOLD CHAIN
  One knob: TONE. Turn it up for a warmer, thicker, shinier sound. INPUT pushes harder; with LINK on,
  the output comes down by the same amount so the level stays put. HUM adds a whisper of hiss.

IVORIES
  For piano and keys. VOICING up = brighter and more present, down = darker and fuller. SLAP adds a soft
  stereo slap, THICKEN two drifting copies left and right; each has its own LEVEL.

HIGH ROLLER
  Five bands, each with its own GAIN: how much of a squeezed, rounded copy of that band is added
  (the quiet detail comes up; 0 = almost nothing). WIDTH spreads that band (1 = as it was, 0 = mono,
  3 = very wide). SOLO plays only the soloed bands, BYPASS takes that band's copy out. XOVER knobs
  move the band edges. DRY is the untouched sound (all the way down = off). SNAP up = crisper
  hits, down = smoother.

THE MIDDLEMAN
  CENTER and SIDES are two faders: the middle (vocal, kick, bass) and the sides. LOW, HIGH and SNAP
  say which fader the lows (under 200 Hz), the highs (over 4 kHz) and the hits follow: 0 = CENTER,
  100 = SIDES. So: pull CENTER down to take a vocal out, then turn LOW up to keep the bass. ALL is
  the overall level. The CENTER readout says how much of what comes out is in the middle.

DOUBLE CROSS
  Makes a mono sound stereo by sending each frequency a bit left or right, sweeping back and forth
  across the spectrum; added back to mono it is exactly the original. SPREAD = how wide (1 = the
  space between the speakers). COMBS = how many times it swings; SWEEP FOCUS / SWEEP SPACING bunch them up
  around one frequency (keep SWEEP SPACING under about 0.6). FINE SHIFT balances the picture. Under FREQ, BASS
  SPREAD takes over (0 keeps the bass in the middle). KIND SPLIT uses CURVE (triangle to square).
  INPUT, WIDTH, AIM, POLARITY and SWAP prepare what comes in. RESET clears the peak readouts.
  It delays the sound by 43 ms (DAWs make up for it).

LOW RIDER
  Adds a new low octave under the bass. RANGE picks the bass it listens to, NEW OCTAVE is the new octave,
  LOWS the original lows, DRY the untouched sound. NEW OCTAVE and LOWS above 0 dB start to saturate.
  LINE UP lines the original up with the new low end. It delays the sound by 70 ms.

UNDERTOW
  Subs from the bass: OCTAVE DOWN an octave down, TWO DOWN two. LOWEST / HIGHEST pick the bass it follows.
  FOLLOW 0 keeps the subs steady, 100 lets them follow every note. MIX 50 = original and subs in
  full; DRIVE roughens the subs; SUB IN MIDDLE puts one sub in the middle.

DEEP POCKETS
  Bass that comes through on phones and laptops: under FREQUENCY the bass is (partly) replaced by
  its harmonics. REAL BASS and OVERTONES set the mix. DECAY near -9 = richer, near -24 = more
  natural. RATIO lifts the quiet bass notes' harmonics, SPEED is how fast. MONITOR lets you hear
  just the overtones or just the bass being replaced.

LOW PROFILE
  The quick version: FREQUENCY, AMOUNT (how much harmonics), REAL BASS in or out, GAIN.

THE FIXER
  Three bands that only turn down when they get nasty: MUD (boxy lows), HARSH (the bite) and FIZZ
  (esses, hiss). AMOUNT up = it starts on quieter sound and takes more off (at most 18 dB); the number
  over each band shows what it takes. FREQ moves the band inside its range. LISTEN plays one band alone
  so you can find the spot, BYPASS switches a band off. INPUT and OUTPUT trim the level.

DEAD DROP
  A delay that can be clean or dirty. TIME in ms, or a NOTE when SYNC is HOST or BPM; TAP twice to set
  it. SPLIT TIMES gives the right side its own time. FEEDBACK past 100 % builds up (the vintage stage
  holds it in). PING PONG bounces left / right. HI-PASS and LO-PASS darken or thin the repeats (TIE
  FILTERS moves both). MOD wobbles the time, VINTAGE 1-4 adds grit and a little hiss, LO-RES lowers the
  sample rate. MIX, OUTPUT.

SIX SHOOTER
  Six echoes you place one by one. Each TAP: ON, DELAY (how late), GAIN, PLACE (where it sits left to
  right) and its own EQ. DRY is the untouched sound. TEMPO: HOST follows the song, MANUAL uses BPM (or
  TAP it in); GRID with LOCK puts the taps on notes. WOBBLE swings the delay times. FEEDBACK sends the
  echoes round again (MODE: ALL TAPS, or OWN DELAY), with its own FILTER. RESET clears the held peaks.

BACK ALLEY
  The cheap-room sound: an ECHO (TIME in ms or a NOTE, FEEDBACK, PING PONG, LEVEL) and a SPRING or
  PLATE reverb (LENGTH, LEVEL), each with its ON. HI-PASS and LO-PASS narrow everything, MONO squeezes
  it toward the middle (10 = as it was), MIX blends it in. The worn tone and wobble are built in.

DRIVE-BY
  A sound driving past you. PASS TIME = how long the whole pass takes, CLOSEST AT = when it's nearest,
  START / END = where it begins and ends, CLOSE = how near it passes, WAY = which direction. MODE LOOP
  passes again and again; ONCE goes once per TRIGGER (SOUND: when the sound starts; BUTTON: press FIRE).
  GAIN, PAN, PITCH, AIR LOSS and REVERB set how real it is (100 = as in life), each with ON; REVERB
  LENGTH and REVERB TONE shape the room.

BODY DOUBLE
  Makes one take sound like two to five. Each of the four VOICES: ON, GAIN, PAN, DELAY, DETUNE,
  FEEDBACK, OCTAVE (an octave down) and MOD (a wobble: DEPTH, RATE). DRY is the original; IN TIME
  makes 0 ms mean together. FEED SPLIT feeds voices 1 / 3 from the left and 2 / 4 from the right.
  LOWEST (20 or 80 Hz) = the lowest note it detunes cleanly. LOW and HIGH shelves shape the voices.
  RESET clears the held peaks.

HUSTLE
  One wobble, three moves: LEVEL, PITCH and SWING (where it sits), each with ON, depth and PHASE.
  RATE x MULTIPLIER is the speed; SYNC HOST locks it to one cycle a beat of the song. WAVE changes the
  shape. GAIN and MIX at the end.

SWERVE
  The jet-plane swoosh. DELAY = where the sweep sits (0.1-0.5 ms phaser-like, 0.5-3 ms flanger, more =
  chorus-like), DEPTH = how far it swings, RATE = how fast (SYNC HOST: sweeps per beat). WAVE SINE is
  smooth, TRIANGLE turns hard at the ends. TIE keeps the pitch wobble the same when you change RATE or
  DEPTH. HOLD stops the sweep where it is. STEREO sets the right side's sweep behind the left (180 = they
  move opposite). FEEDBACK makes it ring (LOOP FLIP for the hollow version), MIX 50 = the deepest swoosh
  (WET FLIP for the other set of notches). FILTER LOW-PASS or HIGH-PASS on the swept sound. TAPE lets the
  sweep pass through zero like two tape machines (the whole sound is then late by DELAY). GAIN at the end.

COLD CASE
  Moving notches that dig holes in the sound, plus a loop that makes them ring. PAIRS = how many notches
  (HALF adds one more at the top), DEPTH = how deep, FLIP turns the holes into bumps, STEREO pulls the
  right side's notches apart from the left, LOW CUT keeps the bass out. The sweep: RUN (on / paused),
  WAVE, RATE x MULTIPLIER (sweeps per beat), TEMPO HOST or its own BPM, LOW and HIGH = where it sweeps
  (set them equal and it stands still). The loop: LOOP on, DECAY = how long it rings, FLIP, DELAY,
  DENSITY (the right side's echoes later), HI TAME (darker), LOOP FILTER. The wet sound: HIGH CUT,
  WIDTH, ROTATE, PRE-DELAY, WET MODE (STEREO, MONO, MONO FLIP), MIX, GAIN.

MOUTHPIECE
  A vocal leveler in one move. PRESS down = the voice is held steadier and comes forward (the level is
  made up for you). CEILING = the most that ever comes out. GATE brings the quiet room noise between
  lines down (all the way left = off). TOTAL shows how much it's working; RESET clears the peaks.

SLINGER
  For guitar. THRESHOLD down = more even (the harder it works the stronger it gets; it lets go by itself,
  slowly after long chords, quickly after single notes). ATTACK longer = the pick comes through.
  GAIN = the level out (high settings push the output limiter: watch the LIMITER readout).

STREET TEAM
  A whole channel on one plate. INPUT first (all the way down = off), then the four-band EQ: each band
  ON, TYPE, FREQ, GAIN, and Q for bells (band 1 can be a HIGH-PASS, band 4 a LOW-PASS). Then the GATE
  (OFF all the way down; FLOOR = how far it closes; G ATTACK / G RELEASE) and the compressor (COMP
  threshold, RATIO - under 1 it expands the loud parts instead -, C ATTACK, C RELEASE), and OUTPUT.

BIG HOUSE
  Twelve kinds of space: TYPE (halls, a room, a chamber, a sanctuary, two plates, BACKWARDS that swells
  up to each hit, GATED that holds then shuts, SHAPED, ECHO ROOM, RINGING). TIME = how long the tail lasts,
  SIZE, DIFFUSION (more = the early reflections feed a smoother tail), DECAY under 3.5 cuts the tail short
  (gated / shaped sounds), PRE-DELAY (minus: the dry waits instead), EARLY and REVERB levels (all the way
  down = off), MIX. LOW / HIGH HZ with their X: the lows or highs last longer (over 1) or shorter (under 1).
  EQ LO / EQ HI shape the tail (all the way down = a cut). VARIATION changes the early reflections a bit.

COIL
  A spring reverb. TANK picks one of seven (DUO = two springs, grittier; TRIO = three, fuller),
  LENGTH how long it rings. DRIVE pushes the sound into the springs (hotter = grittier), HEADROOM
  how soon the peaks get rounded (quiet sounds stay the same level). PRE-DELAY waits before the
  springs. TREBLE and BASS shape the reverb only. MIX, OUTPUT; MONO puts the same reverb on both sides.

THE MOLD and THE FORGER
  Copy the sound of a hardware EQ (or any outboard box) and use it on every track.
  Wire it up: an output of your interface (3 or higher) into the box's input, the box's output back into
  an input of your interface. In the rack app: put THE MOLD in a slot, set SEND (the output that feeds
  the box) and RETURN (the input it comes back on), and switch TRANSMIT on: it sends a quiet hiss-like
  test signal through the box. RECEIVING lights when it hears its own signal come back; LOOP shows the
  round trip. Lower HEADROOM's number if the clip lights come on (CLEAR CLIPS resets them).
  Then put THE FORGER on any track (or in the rack): in CAPTURE it follows the box live, so turn the
  box's knobs and hear the copy change. HOLD keeps the curve (it is saved with your session, so you can
  switch the box off). ADD puts the box's new moves on top of what is held. GAIN sets the level, TRIM
  shows how far under 0 dBFS the peaks are, CLEAR goes back to flat, SCALE zooms the curve.
  In a DAW: put THE MOLD on a track whose output goes to the box and whose input comes back from it
  (its own output is the test signal); only one MOLD is listened to at a time.

GRAND LARCENY
  An electric grand piano. In the rack: put it in a slot and play a MIDI keyboard (any keyboard
  plugged into the PC is picked up within a few seconds; its sustain pedal works), or click the 61
  keys on the plate (LATCH makes a click hold a key until you click it again). In a DAW it loads as an
  instrument on a MIDI track. Whatever is already playing through that rack slot keeps playing.
  KEYS: VEL CURVE (plus = you have to hit harder to get loud), FORMANT (moves the tone's body up or
  down without changing the pitch), TUNE (cents). MIX: MAIN (the strings), KEY UP (the damper landing
  when you let go), ACTION (the keys' thump), PEDAL (the pedal's own sound), SQUEEZE (a compressor mixed
  in), SYMPATHY (the other strings ringing along while the pedal is down). TONE: BASS, MID, TREBLE.
  VOLUME (7 = as it comes), LIMIT keeps it under -0.3 dBFS. EFFECTS turns all of these on or off:
  TREMOLO, AUTO PAN and PHASER (RATE: SYNC to the song with NOTE, or FREE in HZ; DEPTH; the phaser's MIX),
  CHORUS (DEPTH), REVERB (MIX, PRE-DELAY, TIME, DAMP = how fast the highs die). PEDAL is the sustain pedal.
  The piano is our own (made by the plug-in, not recorded).

FINGERPRINT
  A convolution reverb: it plays your sound through the "fingerprint" of a space (its impulse
  response). SPACE picks one of sixteen; the RT60 window shows how long that reverb lasts. START skips
  the front of it, LENGTH cuts it shorter (6 = all of it), DECAY makes it die faster (1 = as it is).
  MIX 100 = reverb only (for a send), lower = some of the dry sound. DIRECT adds the space's own direct
  sound. PRE-DELAY waits before the reverb; DRY DELAY holds the dry sound back instead. CPU LOW drops the
  part of the reverb you can't hear (60 dB down). CROSSTALK lets each side feed the other's reverb.
  OUTPUT. When you change the space, READY goes out for a moment while it is made, then it fades in.
  The original loads recorded spaces; ours are made by the plug-in.

SPEAKEASY
  Puts the sound in a real room. ROOM SIZE = how big the room is, DISTANCE = how far away the sound
  stands (closer = a longer gap before the first reflections and more direct sound). With REAL ROOM on,
  the direct, early and reverb levels and the reverb's start all follow the room and the distance, like
  walking back in a real space; turn it off to set PRE-DELAY yourself. ROOM SHAPE changes the early
  reflections (higher = more of them, denser). BALANCE = more room, less direct, same loudness.
  DECAY = how long the tail lasts, DENSITY = how fast it thickens. TAIL DULL and EARLY DULL take highs off
  the reverb and the early reflections above DULL ABOVE. LOW HZ / LOW X and HIGH HZ / HIGH X: the lows or
  highs last longer (over 1) or shorter (under 1). EARLY TAKE / TAIL TAKE give a slightly different room.
  DIRECT, EARLY and REVERB switch each part on or off, each with its own level. INPUT all the way down = off.

THE INFORMANT
  Talk or sing into it and its own synth "speaks" your words. Play the synth on the 48 KEYS (up to
  eight at once): HOLD = a key sounds while you hold it (drag across for a glide), LATCH = a click
  keeps it on until you click it again. SOUND picks one of ten synth sounds, TUNE moves the whole synth
  (A = 440 Hz normally), SPREAD fans the notes across left and right. CARRIER can also be your track's
  left / right / stereo instead of the synth; MODULATOR picks which side of the input does the talking.
  NOISE adds whispery breath (FILTER and FREQ shape it). SQUEEZE evens out quiet and loud words, FORMANT
  makes the voice smaller or bigger, LAG trades crisp words for a smoother sound, HOLD keeps the last
  word's shape in the pauses. The EQ shapes the vocoded sound. The MIXER blends CARRIER, MODULATOR,
  NOISE and VOCODED (-60 = off), each with SOLO and MUTE. MIDI playing is not possible in the rack.

STRAPPED
  A guitar desk. MODE: CLEAN TONE, EDGE or HIGH GAIN (each its own tone; BYPASS = none). RE-AMP runs it
  through an amp for that mode. SENSITIVITY is the level in, BALANCE tips it left or right, OUTPUT the
  level out. Then six faders, each with a STYLE (OFF switches it out): BASS and TREBLE (which frequency),
  COMPRESS (LIGHT / MEDIUM / HEAVY, the fader is how hard), REVERB (room size), DELAY (a note of the
  tempo) and PITCH (how wide the doubled spread is) - those three faders are how much.

SAFE HOUSE
  For checking a mix on headphones: it sounds like two speakers in a room in front of you. ROOM = how
  much of the room, SPEAKER ANGLE = how wide the speakers sit (60 is the usual triangle), HEAD TURN =
  turn your head (+ = to the right). HEAD SIZE (around) and EAR TO EAR (over the top) match it to your
  own head. MUTE L / MUTE R switch a speaker off. It is for listening only: switch it off before you export.

TORCH
  A guitar amp and two miked cabinets. TYPE picks the amp (STRAIGHT IN and PURE up to FIRE, LEAD and
  VENOM); DRIVE is how hard it's driven; BASS / MID / TREBLE and PRESENCE shape it; MASTER is its
  volume (all 0..10, 5 = the middle). AMP off = only the cabinets. Each cabinet path (A, B) has its CAB
  (NONE = no cabinet and no mic), MIC (dynamic, condenser or ribbon, on or off the speaker's centre),
  VOLUME (0 = off) and PAN; PHASE B flips B (try it when the two sound thin together). ROUTING DUAL
  feeds both cabinets the amp; STEREO sends the left side to A and the right to B (pan them apart).
  The old GATE and ROOM are gone: the original has neither.

HOT WIRE
  A guitar head and its cabinets. AMP picks one of five, CHANNEL CLEAN / DRIVE / LEAD how hard it
  breaks up, GAIN how hard you hit it. FOCUS (0 = off) lifts one band, from low mids up to bite. GATE
  (OFF all the way down) quiets the hiss between notes; GATE SPEED is how fast it closes. BASS, MIDDLE,
  TREBLE, VOLUME; DEPTH (low thump) and PRESENCE (top edge) are the power amp's. CABINET picks a pair of
  takes of one box: CAB TONE mixes the darker and the brighter one. ROOM adds the room's first
  reflections. CAB OFF if you use your own cabinet. The output never goes past -0.1 dBFS: CLIPS counts
  the times it had to stop a peak (CLEAR resets it).

BOTTOM FEEDER
  A bass rig: the amp and the DI (the clean direct sound). BLEND mixes them (0 = all amp, 10 = all DI).
  DI TONE (0 = clean) adds growl to the DI, DI LEVEL, DI FLIP if the two fight. DRIVE: a pedal before the
  amp (FAT, TIGHT, SHARP) with its TONE, GAIN and MIX. VOICE 1 / 2 / 3: a mid lift from narrow to wide.
  GAIN, BASS, MID, HI MID, HIGH. CAB TONE, ROOM, CAB OFF and the output limiter work as on HOT WIRE.

THE RACKET
  A pedalboard, one pedal at a time. Click a pedal at the top; its own knobs and switches come up
  underneath. For a chain of pedals, put several RACKETs in a row. On the left: INPUT (STEREO, MONO, or
  MONO > WIDE: summed to mono, then the stereo pedals spread it) and INPUT GAIN.
  DRIVE: OVERDRIVE (warm), HARD CLIP (BODY scoops the mids), FUZZ (SUSTAIN = how hard), RASP (a
    buzzy, peaky one), HEAVY (with LOW, HIGH and a MID you can move). LEVEL 50 = as loud as it gets
    clipped, 100 = 12 dB more.
  MOVEMENT: FLANGE, VIB / TREM (vibrato wobbles the pitch, tremolo the level), AUTO PAN, PHASE, OCT DOWN
    (one and two octaves under the note: play one note at a time), WAH (AUTO follows your playing,
    HAND sits where POSITION puts it), CHORUS, THICKEN (two slightly detuned copies), PITCH RIDE and
    BASS RIDE (POSITION slides the pitch between LOW SHIFT and HIGH SHIFT; automate it in a DAW).
    SYNC ON locks the speed or time to the song's tempo with NOTE.
  SPACE: ECHO (FEEDBACK past about 8.7 keeps building; STEREO bounces the echoes side to side), FLIP
    ECHO (REVERSE plays each piece backwards; PITCH moves the echoes), ROOM, SPRING.
  BASICS: SQUEEZE (a compressor), SLAM (squeeze hard), GATE, CLEAN UP (a gate and a squeeze in one),
    TONE STACK, GRAPHIC (six narrow bands from 125 Hz to 4 kHz), VOLUME (LOWEST and HIGHEST set the
    pedal's travel; LIN or LOG says how it moves between them).

THE SNITCH
  Tuner. Play one note: it shows the NOTE, how many CENTS off (minus = flat, plus = sharp) and the
  PITCH in Hz. MODE: CHROMATIC (any note), GUITAR (E A D G B E), DROP D, OPEN G, OPEN D, OPEN E,
  DADGAD, HALF DOWN, or MY NOTES (only the notes you switch on below; switch on one for a single
  note). It tunes to the nearest of those notes. REFERENCE A moves 440 up or down by up to 6 cents.
  There is no mute (the original has none): bypass the rest of the chain while you tune.

PROMOTION
  Moves the pitch, up to an octave either way. SET BY TRANSPOSE: SEMITONES (named as intervals: +5TH,
  -OCTAVE ...) and CENTS on top; or SET BY FREQUENCY: FROM the pitch it is, TO the pitch you want (Hz).
  MODE: SMOOTH for held notes and vocals, SYNC close to it, TRANSIENT keeps the hits sharp, PUNCHY for
  rhythm parts. It delays the sound by 35 ms (DAWs make up for it). The original can also stretch the
  length of a whole file; a live rack can't, so that part isn't here.

WISEGUYS
  Six backing voices from one singer. Switch a VOICE on, set its PITCH in semitones (3 = minor third,
  4 = major third, 7 = fifth, 12 = octave, minus = below; fractions = cents), FORMANT (moves the
  voice's character: up = smaller, younger; down = bigger), GAIN, PAN and DELAY. DIRECT is the original
  singer (ON, GAIN, DELAY). ANIMATE lets the voices drift apart in time so they don't land together.
  LINK makes the formant follow the pitch (OFF = not at all, FULL = fully, OPPOSITE = the other way;
  the others are in between). MODE and MIN / MAX PITCH tell it what kind of voice it is reading. It
  works on one voice or one instrument line, not chords. It delays the sound by 85 ms (DAWs make up).

MADE MAN
  Pitch correction for one voice. Set ROOT and SCALE (CHROMATIC = every note; MY NOTES = only the notes
  switched on below). SPEED all the way down = the hard, robotic snap; up = gentle and natural. NOTE
  GLIDE is how fast it moves from one note to the next. RATIO sets how far it pulls (100 = all
  the way). FORMANT CORRECTED keeps the voice's character; OFF lets it change with the pitch. SHIFT
  moves everything (-12 = an octave drop). REFERENCE A for songs tuned off 440. MIN / MAX PITCH: the
  range of the voice. SUNG shows the note, PULL how far it moved it. It delays the sound by 85 ms (DAWs
  make up for it) and comes out in mono (one voice). Its twenty scales are ours; the original lists 43.

THE DETECTIVE
  Play the song: after a few seconds it shows the KEY (MAJ / MIN) and keeps listening until the same
  key has held for 30 seconds (or the music stops); then the two OR windows show the next likeliest
  keys. TUNING is where the song's A sits (440 = standard). DETECT stops it, or starts over. EDIT shows
  a key you pick (ROOT, SCALE) instead. SPELL writes the keys with SHARPS or FLATS. (The original can
  send its key to other plug-ins; a rack has no way to do that.)

THE AUDITOR
  A loudness meter for delivering to a standard. The windows: SHORT TERM, LONG TERM (everything since
  RESET), MOMENTARY, RANGE, TRUE PEAK and LIMITER (the most each reached; PEAKS clears them), TRIM (how
  much GAIN would land LONG TERM on TARGET), TIMER, and UNDERS / OVERS (how many times SHORT TERM went
  under SHORT MIN or over SHORT MAX). METHOD EBU (the broadcast standard; short time always 3 s), AVERAGE
  (no relative gate) or DIALOGUE (counts only where speech carries the sound - our simple version).
  WEIGHTING: BS.1770 for loudness, or the LEQ curves. SCALE: LU reads 0 at -23 LUFS. LOW-PASS /
  HIGH-PASS filter only what is measured. GAIN changes the sound itself; LIMITER keeps true peaks under
  TRUE PEAK MAX (no make-up). MEASURE PAUSE stops the counting. The original's TRIM button sets the gain
  for you and it can log to a file; here you read TRIM and set GAIN yourself.

STAKEOUT
  Watches the sound (it passes untouched). The big window shows the frequencies as bars: LF RESOLUTION
  sets how fine the bars are under 250 Hz (40 / 20 / 10 Hz), MODE PEAK (the bars fall back over
  RESPONSE) or RMS (an average over RESPONSE), CHANNELS L / R (two bars per band, left teal, right gold)
  or L+R, WEIGHTING (A, B, C: closer to how loud we hear it), FREEZE holds the picture, MAX LINE shows the
  most each band reached (MAX clears it). The half circle shows where each band sits: straight up = the
  middle, the 45-degree lines = hard left / right, the red zones = out of phase (it may vanish in mono).
  The meters: PEAK L / R, SUM (half of left plus right, as PEAK or RMS, through the weighting) and their
  HOLDs (each resets with its own button), CLIP L / R (CLIPS puts them out).

THE WITNESS
  VU meters for left and right (slow, like the needles on a desk). HEADROOM sets how far under full
  scale 0 VU sits (18 dB is the usual). DISPLAY DELAY holds the needles back (up to 1 s) so meters on
  buses that come out later line up. The CLIP windows light up when a side touches the top; CLEAR puts
  them out. The old peak meters and PEAK HOLD are gone: the original has neither.

LIVE WIRE
  A test signal, off until you switch POWER on (then only the signal comes out). TYPE: PINK noise,
  WHITE noise or a SINE. FREQ (for the sine) and GAIN; QUICK FREQ and QUICK GAIN jump to the usual
  settings (100 Hz / 1 kHz / 10 kHz; -6 / -12 / -20 dB) - back to KNOB to use the knobs. SEND: left,
  right or both. FLIP turns the right side upside down (checks wiring). Start quiet: tones can be loud.
  The old square, saw, triangle and sweep, and ADD, are gone: the original has none of them.

FURNACE
  DRIVE for more tube colour (the level stays about the same), BIAS toward EVEN = warmer.
  TONE darker or brighter after the tube, MIX to blend.

CATHEDRAL, CHROME, BACKROOM
  DECAY = how many seconds the tail lasts. PRE-DELAY keeps the dry sound clear before the
  reverb starts (20 - 60 ms on vocals). DAMPING makes the tail darker. LOW CUT on the reverb
  keeps the low end clean. For a send-style sound in the rack, keep MIX low (10 - 25 %).

REPEAT OFFENDER
  Pick a NOTE (1/4, 1/8, dotted, triplets) and set BPM to your song's tempo, or FREE and TIME in
  ms. PING-PONG bounces the repeats left and right. LOW / HIGH CUT shape the repeats.

THE CREW, GETAWAY, SMOKESCREEN, SHAKEDOWN, CYCLONE, SHAPESHIFTER
  RATE = how fast, DEPTH = how much. MIX 50 % gives GETAWAY and SMOKESCREEN their deepest sweep.
  CYCLONE: switch SLOW / FAST and listen to it speed up - the horn gets there before the drum.
  SHAPESHIFTER: ENVELOPE + opens the filter when the music gets louder (funk), - closes it.

UNDERGROUND
  OCTAVE: a new sub an octave under the bass. HARMONICS: the bass's overtones, so the low end
  comes through on phones and laptops. Start with AMOUNT low - it adds up fast.

SPOTLIGHT
  Top to bottom of a vocal chain: HIGH-PASS, BODY, COMPRESS (one knob, with makeup), DE-ESS
  (one knob), PRESENCE, AIR. Everything at 0 = untouched.

THE ACCOUNTANT (measures only, changes nothing)
  INTEGRATED = the whole song since RESET (play it all the way through). SHORT-TERM = the last
  3 seconds, MOMENTARY = the last 0.4 s. LOUDNESS RANGE = how much it moves (LU).
  TRUE PEAK MAX = the highest peak so far. TARGET: -14 (Spotify / YouTube), -16 (Apple Music),
  -11 / -9 (loud); TO TARGET shows how far off you are (+ = louder than the target).

Knobs: drag up / down (hold Shift for fine), mouse wheel, double-click = back to default.

OUTPUT (top right) picks the sound card; click it to step through them. "ASIO: ..." outputs are your
audio interface's own ASIO driver - usually the lowest delay; the buffer size is set in the driver's own
control panel. The others are Windows outputs, used in exclusive mode - other programs go quiet while it
plays. With nothing picked yet it tries ASIO first, then Windows' default output.

Your chain, output and folders are remembered in %APPDATA%\HEM-Elite-Rack.
The VST3 plug-ins (all of them, named HEM ...) are in Common Files\VST3\HEM-Elite-Rack.vst3
for any DAW that loads VST3.

Needs: Windows 10 / 11 64-bit, a processor with AVX2 (Intel 2013+, AMD 2015+), OpenGL 3.3.
